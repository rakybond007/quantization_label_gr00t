Compression is the default: answer YES when the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, broad sweeps, pushing a single surface or panel shut, OR rotating a knob, faucet, or spout in place (a steady turn that stays in one spot is free motion even though the gripper rests on it). Answer NO when the gripper is in delicate contact: grasping an object, inserting or aligning it precisely, when the gripper is hooked on a door handle or drawer pull and dragging that whole panel open along its path (a hinged door swinging out toward the robot, or a drawer sliding out), OR when TWO door panels are in the frame closing toward each other — their free edges approaching a shared center seam to meet and align (the final mating of two swinging leaves is a precise terminal contact, not a free push). The presence of a SECOND converging leaf is the decisive signal: if two panels are coming together at the center, answer NO through that approach and meeting even though it superficially looks like pushing shut. The push-shut YES applies ONLY to a SINGLE panel being pushed against its frame with no second panel converging. A knob, faucet, or spout that merely spins in place is NOT a panel being hauled open — keep turning it YES. Decide from the camera views, not the task name.
