Default answer is YES — compress. Say YES for almost all motion: reaching toward an object before contact, lifting, transporting or carrying a grasped object across open space, hovering, sweeping, and retracting — these are the bulk of every episode and compress safely. Also say YES when an object is being lowered or released over a WIDE, open, forgiving target — a drop into a basket, bin, or bowl where the landing area is large and no exact alignment with another specific object is required; that release does not need precision and should compress. Say NO ONLY at these delicate contact instants, judged from the wrist (eye-in-hand) close-up: (1) the moment the gripper fingers are straddling and closing on an object to grasp it, and (2) the final slow lowering/alignment when an object is being seated into a TIGHT or EXACT spot — nesting against, stacking on top of, inserting into a narrow slot, or settling into a stated spatial relation with another specific object (left/right/front/back/on) where a small placement error would miss. Keep the distinction sharp: a drop into a roomy container is YES; a precise inter-object placement is NO. Everything else — including all travel between these instants — is YES. When motion looks like open-space transport or a drop into a forgiving target rather than fine inter-object contact, prefer YES. Decide from the camera views, never the task name.
