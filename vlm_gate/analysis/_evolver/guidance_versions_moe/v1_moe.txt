Answer NO only at the brief fine-manipulation instants of handling an object: (1) closing the gripper on an object to pick it up, especially when reaching into a confined space (sink basin, microwave interior, cabinet, shelf) to grasp it; (2) lowering and releasing the held object at its destination — this is a brief NO at the release instant for an open counter or a wide cabinet/shelf, but a LONGER, stricter NO spanning the whole descent through release when the destination is a SINK BASIN (lowering over the drain or standing water, clearing the rim) or a STOVETOP (settling onto burners or grates), where the object can tip, roll, or must clear an edge; (3) inserting or aligning a HELD OBJECT into a tight slot or receptacle where millimeters matter. Carrying the object through open space BETWEEN pickup and release is YES.
Learned exception: when the gripper is holding a handle and pulling a door or drawer OPEN (it swings or slides open as the arm moves), answer NO during the opening pull. Pushing a door or drawer CLOSED is forgiving and stays YES.
Operating a control without picking anything up — pressing a button (even a small machine button), flipping a switch, turning a knob or dial — is a forgiving tap or turn and stays YES. EXCEPTION: grasping and turning a FAUCET handle/lever at a sink basin (controlling water flow over the drain) is finicky and matters for success — answer NO during the grasp-and-turn of a sink faucet. Knobs, dials, switches and buttons on appliances (stove, microwave, coffee machine) stay YES.
Otherwise answer YES — reaching through open space, carrying a grasped object, retracting, and broad arm sweeps are all fine to compress.
For genuinely borderline moments, answer with real uncertainty rather than forcing a side; a confidence threshold makes the final call.
The wrist (eye-in-hand) camera is mounted on the gripper, so objects routinely fill it; that closeness alone is NOT a reason for NO.
Decide from the scene, not the task name.
