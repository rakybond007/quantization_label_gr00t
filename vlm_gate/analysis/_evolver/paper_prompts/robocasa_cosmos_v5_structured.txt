DECISION POLICY (learned): Compression is the DEFAULT — answer YES unless you can actually see one of the delicate instants listed under NO.

YES — compressible situations:
1. Free-space motion: reaching toward an object, transporting a grasped object, retracting, or broad sweeps.
2. Operating a mechanism that is BOLTED IN PLACE while carrying nothing: pressing a button, flipping or turning a knob or dial, rotating a faucet handle, or pushing a door or drawer shut. Pair-averaging cannot misplace a fixed fixture, and there is no carried object to drop. In the wrist (eye-in-hand) view these read as an EMPTY gripper, or a gripper resting flat against a panel or control — not an object clutched between the fingers. An empty gripper on a fixed control is a strong YES even when the arm is barely moving.

NO — delicate instants only:
(a) Grasp-closure: the moment the gripper closes on an object or a handle.
(b) Confined placement: the final approach that lowers, aligns, inserts, or releases a CARRIED object into a confined spot — settling it onto a surface, into a basin, onto a burner, or through a narrow opening.
(c) Constrained pull-open: pulling a door or drawer OPEN, where the grasped handle is under load and the arm must track the hinge or slide (pushing it CLOSED is YES).

One narrow caution inside the YES set: when two door panels must be swung shut together (a double door), the final moment where the two panels meet and latch is delicate — lean NO only there.

VISUAL NOTES: Everything else stays YES. Decide from the camera views, not the task name. Use the wrist view to spot the actual grasp-closure or carried-object placement instant; objects always look close in it, so closeness alone is not a precision cue — and an empty gripper acting on a fixed control is never a precision cue.
