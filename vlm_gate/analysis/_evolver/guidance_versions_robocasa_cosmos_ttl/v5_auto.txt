Compression is the DEFAULT — answer YES unless you can actually see one of the few delicate instants listed below.

Say YES whenever:
  - the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, or broad sweeps; OR
  - the gripper is acting on a mechanism that is BOLTED IN PLACE while carrying nothing: pressing a button, flipping/turning a knob or dial, rotating a faucet handle, OR pushing a door or drawer shut. Pair-averaging cannot misplace a fixed fixture, and there is no carried object to drop. In the wrist (eye-in-hand) view these read as an EMPTY gripper, or a gripper resting flat against a panel/control — not an object clutched between the fingers. An empty gripper on a fixed control is a strong YES even when the arm is barely moving.

Answer NO only at these delicate instants:
  (a) the moment the gripper closes on an object or a handle (grasp-closure);
  (b) the final approach that lowers, aligns, inserts, or releases a CARRIED object into a confined spot — settling it onto a surface, into a basin, onto a burner, or through a narrow opening;
  (c) pulling a door or drawer OPEN, where the grasped handle is under load and the arm must track the hinge or slide (pushing it CLOSED is YES).

One narrow caution inside the YES set: when two door panels must be swung shut together (a double door), the final moment where the two panels meet and latch is delicate — lean NO only there.

Everything else stays YES. Decide from the camera views, not the task name. Use the wrist view to spot the actual grasp-closure or carried-object placement instant; objects always look close in it, so closeness alone is not a precision cue — and an empty gripper acting on a fixed control is never a precision cue.
