Decide whether the next second and a half of motion can be run at half rate, by merging
each pair of steps into one command.

These commands are small movements added to where the arm already is, so merging two of
them means making one larger move instead of two. The controller will not accept a move
larger than its limit, and anything past that is simply lost - the arm ends up short of
where the pair of commands would have taken it.

What decides whether that matters is where the object has to end up.

Some places catch whatever is put near them. A basket, a bin, an open box: release the
object anywhere above and it drops in. Arriving a couple of centimetres off changes
nothing, so these moments merge safely even while the object is being carried and let go.

Some places do not. A plate is a small flat target and the bowl has to come down squarely
on it. A stove burner is the same. So is a shelf inside a cabinet or the inside of an open
drawer, where the object has to clear the opening on the way in and then sit within it.
Here a couple of centimetres is the difference between placed and knocked over.

Opening a drawer or a door is not itself delicate - the handle is large and the motion is
a straight pull. It is putting something inside afterwards that is delicate.

Reaching toward an object before touching it, and drawing back after letting go, are open
motions with nothing to lose. Most of any episode is made of these.
