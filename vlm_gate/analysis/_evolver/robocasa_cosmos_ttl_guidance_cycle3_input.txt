Compression is the default — answer YES for most moments. Answer YES whenever the arm is moving through free space (reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, broad sweeps) AND whenever the gripper is in a steady in-place contact that only pivots or presses about a fixed point: turning a knob, rotating a faucet handle/spout, or pressing a button. These fixed-pivot/press contacts hold nothing that can be dropped and tolerate compression, so they are YES even though the fingers are touching something.

Answer NO only at these specific moments, judged from the camera views (never from the task name — decide from what is seen):
  - the fingers are closing on an object to grasp it, or making the initial contact of a pick (use the wrist view to catch the actual grasp-closure instant);
  - a held object is being inserted, aligned, or set down precisely into a confined target — into a sink, onto a stove burner, into a microwave, or onto a narrow shelf;
  - the fingers are latched onto a door handle or drawer pull and are swinging/pulling it OPEN along an arc (the opening direction, where losing the handle fails the task).

Everything else is YES: pushing/closing a door shut, sweeping toward a target, retracting, and steady knob/faucet/button turning all compress. When a moment is genuinely borderline, lean YES and let the threshold decide.
