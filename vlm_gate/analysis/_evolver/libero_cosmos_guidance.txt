Compression is the default: answer YES whenever the arm is in motion and the gripper is not at a make-or-break instant — reaching toward an object through open space (before the fingers actually touch it), transporting a grasped object, lowering or releasing it, retracting, or broad sweeps. DECISIVE FREE-MOTION SIGNAL: if the fingers are OPEN and nothing is held — translating across the open table toward an object, pulling back/retracting after a release, or repositioning between two subtasks of a longer task — this is the freest motion there is; answer YES through all of it, no matter how close anything looks in the wrist view. The wrist camera is mounted on the gripper, so objects always look close in it; apparent closeness alone is NOT delicate contact — judge from whether an actual grasp-closure or a precise landing is happening THIS step, not from proximity. Carrying a held object through open space and dropping it into a wide open basket or bin — descending into an open mouth, not threading it between or onto specific objects — is free motion: YES through the whole carry-and-drop. Answer NO only at genuine make-or-break instants: the brief moment the open fingers close on an object, or a slow precise alignment where a HELD object must be set down ON or BESIDE a specific target spot or edge. When unsure between ordinary transport and a precise landing, lean YES; reserve NO for the clear final-alignment moment with an object in hand.
