Default answer is YES — compress. Say YES for almost all motion: reaching/approaching toward an object before contact, lifting a just-grasped object, transporting or carrying it across open space, hovering, sweeping, and retracting. These are the bulk of every episode and they compress safely. IMPORTANT: the wrist (eye-in-hand) camera is mounted on the gripper, so objects look close in it for many frames — mere closeness is NOT a delicate instant. Do not answer NO just because the object appears near; keep answering YES through the whole approach and the lift right after grasp. Say NO ONLY at these two delicate instants: (1) the exact frames where the gripper fingers are visibly straddling an object AND actively clamping shut on it (the closure instant itself, not the travel leading up to it), judged from the wrist close-up; and (2) the final slow lowering/alignment when an object is being seated into a TIGHT inter-object relation — nesting, stacking, dropping into a slot/hole, or a stated precise spatial relation with another object. A placement into a loose/open target (a basket, an open area, a general 'on top of' with clearance) is NOT tight seating — that descent is YES. Everything else, including all travel between these instants, is YES. When motion looks like open-space transport or approach rather than the actual clamp/seating moment, prefer YES. Decide from the camera views, never the task name.
