Compression is the default: answer YES when the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, or broad sweeps — AND also when the gripper is steadily actuating a FIXED, anchored control without carrying a free object: pressing a button, flipping a switch, turning or rotating a knob, dial, lever, or faucet handle IN PLACE, or pushing a single door or drawer shut. These are steady, non-delicate contacts and stay compressible — and note that the fingers CLOSING around such a fixed control in order to turn or actuate it is part of this steady actuation, NOT a grasp, so it is still YES. Answer NO only at a genuinely delicate moment: the instant the fingers close on a FREE, liftable object to pick it up, or while a held object is being threaded, inserted, or precisely aligned into a tight target or receptacle (including hauling a panel open along its hinge/slide). The grasp-protect applies only to closing on a movable object that will be lifted away — never to closing on something fixed that stays put. Borderline contact should read as genuinely uncertain — let the threshold decide, do not force NO. Decide from the camera views, not the task name.
