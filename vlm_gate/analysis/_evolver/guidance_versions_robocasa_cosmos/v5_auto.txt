Compression is the default: answer YES when the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, or broad sweeps. Also answer YES, with real confidence, whenever the gripper is steadily actuating a control that stays FIXED to the scene and is NOT being carried away: pressing a button, flipping a switch, turning a knob or small dial, rotating a faucet handle, swinging a spout, or pushing a single door or drawer shut. This includes the gripper working a small control mounted on a stationary appliance face — a stove/oven dial, a microwave keypad or button panel — that the hand merely nudges or rotates in place; the control does not travel with the hand, so this is steady, non-delicate contact = YES. Closing the fingers AROUND such an anchored control (a knob, dial, faucet handle, spout, switch, or a door/drawer handle you are pushing closed) is YES — it is NOT the grasp moment, because nothing is about to be lifted free. Lowering or releasing a CARRIED object onto a broad open landing — an open countertop, a stovetop, an open shelf, or down into a cavity that is plainly WIDER than the object (a wide microwave interior) — is free placement with clearance to spare and is YES; the object is set down, not threaded in. Answer NO only at a genuinely delicate moment: the instant the fingers close to LIFT a free object up off a surface, or while a held object is being threaded, inserted, or precisely aligned into a TIGHT or recessed target — a narrow slot, a snug receptacle, or set down into a sink basin among fixtures — or while hauling a panel open along its hinge/slide. Borderline contact should read as genuinely uncertain — let the threshold decide, do not force NO. Decide from the camera views, not the task name.
