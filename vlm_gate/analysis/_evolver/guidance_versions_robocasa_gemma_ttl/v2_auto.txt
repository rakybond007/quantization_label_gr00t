Compression is the default: answer YES when the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object toward a target, retracting, broad sweeps, or pushing a door or drawer closed. A steady in-place TWIST of a knob, faucet handle, or spout also counts as free motion (YES): it is a smooth continuous rotation, not a precision grasp, and the object is not being carried anywhere. Answer NO only when the gripper is in delicate or constrained contact: (a) grasping an object, inserting/aligning it precisely, or lowering and releasing a held object DOWN between the walls of a confined recessed basin such as a sink (a walled, cluttered descent where a small error collides with the rim or contents — this is precise placement, not free transport); or (b) closed on a handle, knob, or drawer-pull and drawing a door or drawer OPEN, so the object is translating with the gripper along a fixed hinge or slide arc — this constrained pull-open is not free space, even though the arm appears to be sweeping. Note the difference in (b): translating the object along a hinge is NO, but merely twisting a knob or spout in place (no translation) is YES. Closing a single door or drawer with a push is still free space (YES). Placing onto an open surface such as a counter, stovetop, cabinet shelf, or microwave tray is free transport (YES) — only the confined recessed-basin descent is protected. Decide from the camera views, not the task name; use the wrist view to confirm whether the gripper is actually engaged on a handle, twisting in place, or descending into a walled basin versus moving in open air.
