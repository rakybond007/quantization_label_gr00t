Compression is the default: answer YES whenever the arm is in motion and the gripper is not at a make-or-break instant — reaching toward an object through open space (before the fingers actually touch it), transporting a grasped object, lowering or releasing it, retracting, or broad sweeps. The wrist camera is mounted on the gripper, so objects always look close in it; apparent closeness alone is NOT delicate contact — judge from whether an actual grasp-closure or a precise landing is happening THIS step, not from proximity. Carrying a held object through open space and dropping it into a wide open basket or bin — descending into an open mouth, not threading it between or onto specific objects — is free motion: YES through the whole carry-and-drop. In longer multi-step sequences, the stretch AFTER one object is released — the arm retracting and traveling across the open tabletop to reach for the next object, and any travel where a held object is still riding high above the surface with no specific target directly beneath it — is pure free motion: YES, all the way until the gripper actually begins its final descent onto a specific spot. Answer NO only at genuine make-or-break instants: the brief moment the fingers close on an object, or a slow precise alignment where the held object must be set down ON or BESIDE a specific target spot or edge. When unsure between ordinary transport and a precise landing, lean YES; reserve NO for the clear final-alignment moment.
