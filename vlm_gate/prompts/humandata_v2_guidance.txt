Going faster leaves the arm fewer chances to correct itself along the way. That costs most at the two moments where the object can be lost: when the hand is closing on it, and when it is being brought down into the place it has to end up. Both get worse the less room there is -- letting a cube down into a cup barely wider than itself leaves no slack, while letting the same cube down onto an open plate does.

Most moments are neither. Crossing empty space to reach for something, carrying something with room all round it, or drawing the hand back after letting go -- a longer step changes nothing there. That is the common case.
