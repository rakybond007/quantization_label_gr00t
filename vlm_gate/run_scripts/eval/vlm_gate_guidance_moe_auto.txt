Answer NO only at the brief fine-manipulation instants of handling an object: (1) closing the gripper on an object to pick it up — and when that object sits inside an ENCLOSED CAVITY (microwave interior, sink basin, cabinet/shelf interior), keep answering NO through the whole grasp AND the lift up and out, until the object has fully cleared the rim/opening edge into open space, since it can catch, snag, or tip on the enclosure edge on the way out. Picking an object off an OPEN surface (open counter, open stovetop, an open shelf with nothing overhead) is just the brief grasp instant, then YES. (2) lowering and releasing the held object at its destination — this is a brief NO at the release instant for an open counter or a wide cabinet/shelf, but a LONGER, stricter NO spanning the whole descent through release when the destination is a SINK BASIN (lowering over the drain or standing water, clearing the rim) or a STOVETOP (settling onto burners or grates), where the object can tip, roll, or must clear an edge; (3) inserting or aligning a HELD OBJECT into a tight slot or receptacle where millimeters matter. Carrying the object through open space BETWEEN pickup and release is YES.
Door/drawer OPENING — separate by the motion you see: when the gripper holds a handle and a DRAWER slides straight OUT toward the camera as the arm pulls back, answer NO for the whole pull (the drawer must clear its frame and come to a square stop; over-compressing this loses success). When a SINGLE lone hinged panel is pulled open on its arc by its handle — one door swinging aside, nothing meeting it — answer NO through the pull, since the latch must release cleanly and the single panel's swing is finicky. When TWO panels swing APART away from a shared center seam (a double cabinet opening to both sides at once), that is forgiving and stays YES.
CLOSING is forgiving: pushing a SINGLE door or a drawer CLOSED stays YES. EXCEPTION: when TWO doors swing toward each other to meet and register at a center seam to latch, answer NO through the ENTIRE final convergence — from when the two panels are closing in and beginning to align against one another all the way through the latch, not merely the last instant — since the panels must register squarely against each other.
Operating a control without picking anything up — pressing a button (even a small machine button), flipping a switch, turning a knob or dial — is a forgiving tap or turn and stays YES. EXCEPTION: grasping and turning a FAUCET handle/lever at a sink basin (controlling water flow over the drain) is finicky and matters for success — answer NO during the grasp-and-turn of a sink faucet. Knobs, dials, switches and buttons on appliances (stove, microwave, coffee machine) stay YES.
Otherwise answer YES — reaching through open space, carrying a grasped object, retracting, and broad arm sweeps are all fine to compress.
For genuinely borderline moments, answer with real uncertainty rather than forcing a side; a confidence threshold makes the final call.
The wrist (eye-in-hand) camera is mounted on the gripper, so objects routinely fill it; that closeness alone is NOT a reason for NO.
Decide from the scene, not the task name.
