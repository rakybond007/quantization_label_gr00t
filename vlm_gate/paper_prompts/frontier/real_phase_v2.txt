Compression is the default: answer YES whenever the arm is moving through free space — reaching, transporting a grasped object, retracting, or broad sweeps — and ALSO when the gripper is simply pressing a button or turning a rigidly-mounted knob or dial, where the mechanism is fixed in place and small timing differences cannot break anything.
Holding an object is NOT by itself a reason to answer NO. Once the object is grasped stably and the arm is simply carrying it, the motion is gross transport: answer YES even though the gripper is closed and in contact.
Reserve NO for the brief windows where timing actually matters: the moments just before, during and just after the gripper opens or closes; slow, deliberate alignment onto or into a target (inserting, threading, placing precisely, stacking); and any moment where the arm is decelerating into contact with something it must not disturb.
A useful test: if the arm is moving quickly and its goal is still far away, answer YES. If it is moving slowly while already touching or nearly touching the target, answer NO.
The gripper on this robot is black and can be hard to see against dark backgrounds — judge its state from the wrist camera and from whether an object moves with the hand, not from brightness alone.
